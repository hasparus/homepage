tldr: In 2024 I built bear-fit to make meeting my friends dead simple. Try it at
[bear-fit.haspar.us](https://bear-fit.haspar.us). It works because it asks
"which days could work?", not "which 30-minute intervals are you free three
weeks from now?"

## Backstory

I've been fighting calendars for more than ten years.

My first job and uni ate my calendar, freedom, and ability to grab a beer with a
friend. A day job + day uni, with one of the most challenging majors in town.
Great combo. It made me stop playing League of Legends, so there's that, but my
calendar was **packed**.

Most of my friends' calendars were also packed. Eureka moment. What if I did
**set intersections?!** Just `&` calendars across each other. Simple, right?!

Okay, but what if the users are not corporate slaves and their availability
can't be derived from the events in their calendars?

Tinder was kinda hot back
then<sup><Link id="bf-fnr1" href="#bf-fn1">1</Link></sup>, so I thought _what if
I could swipe time slots?_ Right for green and available, left for gray and
busy. A stack of your friends' avatars so you can see who's free. Long press on
the row to send an invite.

## New World

Six years passed. It's so easy to build something small ~~now~~, I mean, in
September 2024.

I was still fighting calendars. This time with even more people to coordinate.

I built the first version in about 15 hours across 6 evenings, including playing
with val.town Townie, then moving to PartyKit, Y.js and a local editor when
Townie couldn't figure out SSE and I caught myself writing CSS diffs in its
chat.

Later on I fixed some bugs and added some small features. In total it was about
80 hours of work. Still cheap by software standards; nowhere near the cost of
building something serious.

## Lo-fi as in low-fidelity

I used [system.css](https://sakofchit.github.io/system.css), a global
stylesheet, to avoid spending 2 hours picking a font and communicate this is
intentionally not a polished product. Just a useful tool like computers used to
be in 1991.

Contrary to Elon Musk's philosophy: Start small. Or better yet! **Aim low.** Do
something that's deliberately ass.

I'm a sucker for personal malleable software. Been like that since I read the
first blog posts. A few years ago I saw
[Patchwork](https://www.inkandswitch.com/patchwork/notebook/2024-version-control/)
and [Playbit](https://playbit.app/) at Local-First Conf in Berlin. It seems like
with agents and environments like
[Cloudflare OS](https://blog.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-os) it's slowly happening
now.

Two years ago when I built bear-fit, software was already cheap enough you could
build for a few dozen people. It's even cheaper now.

But let's go back to the thing. Keeping the scope small allowed me to keep
bear-fit dumb simple to use.

## Dead simple UX

I built this app to meet up with my friends, some of them **normies**. They use
a work computer, then don't touch it after work. Not the laptop in a coffeeshop
petite bourgeoisie.

If you don't know any normie, the main thing you need to understand about them
is that computers effectively hate them.

If they have a website, they probably bought the domain bundled with hosting and
WordPress at a 500% markup. The internet is brutal to normies, but they're used
to that. Low-tech vendors have been preying on them **for decades**.

I needed the UX to be so straightforward enough people actually bother. Just
click the day. Like an advent calendar. That's it. You can drag to "paint".
There's basically just a mobile UI. Desktop UI is a phone-shaped card in the
middle of the screen.

By simplicity I mean something quantifiable here: the number of clicks to your
goal. I got down to 2:

1. Click the link in a group chat to visit the app.
2. Click the day when you're available or paint the whole week.

{/* TODO: Demo embed with painting on the calendar. One-off UI. A fake "collaborator" cursor paints a span of 3 days Mon-Wed and clicks through the Friday, Sunday. */}

That's the entire guest flow. No account, onboarding, installation, calendar
permissions, or save button. Open the link, mark your days, leave. Everyone sees
the changes immediately.

Creating the calendar takes three clicks: choose start, choose end, press Enter.

You don't have to name the calendar nor yourself if you ain't got time for that.

I could technically get down to 0 clicks in repeated use with about 5 for setup
by piggybacking on Google Calendar but it wouldn't work for all of my friends.

To make something simple for the users, we often have to
[absorb the complexity](https://overreacted.io/what-are-the-react-team-principles/#absorb-the-complexity)
ourselves.

First, we reduce complexity through product judgment and scoping, like realizing
users can't predict hourly availability three weeks out, then cutting hours from
the product.

Then, we eat the irreducible complexity with code. Take up the cross and suffer
random segmentation faults from your dev deps so the user can click rectangles
on the screen.

If we absorb enough pain, the end result can feel like magic.

## Autosave is magic apparently

People repeatedly asked: "How do I save? Did it work? Just like that?"

Real-time multiplayer helps avoid conversations like "yeah, I'm just doing it,
saving in a minute", but for first-time users, it's surprising. It's something
they haven't seen before.
